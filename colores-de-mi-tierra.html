Los colores de mi tierra

Harris Paints, 1991. The seven paint colors the song names, sampled from the video frames of the ad, with every code derived from one master value per color.

Frambuesa piraguaBlanco cocoAmarillo mangóVerde quenepaAzul adoquinesRojo flamboyánTurquesa mar Caribe

Which code to trust

1HEX and RGB

The most accurate. The video stores color as BT.709 Y'CbCr, and BT.709 uses the same primaries and white point as sRGB. Decoding it gives sRGB directly, so HEX is the source data rounded to 8 bits.

2HSL and HSB

The RGB numbers re-expressed as hue, saturation and lightness. Nothing is added or lost beyond rounding.

3Lab, LCH, OKLCH

Exact math from sRGB into device-independent spaces. Give the Lab value to a paint store, since their color-matching spectrophotometers read in Lab.

4CMYK

The least accurate. CMYK depends on ink, paper and press, so the numbers change with the ICC profile. ΔE shows how far the print lands from the screen color. Below 2 is hard to see.

These are the colors as the video recorded them. The ad went from 1991 film to tape to a YouTube encode, and each step can shift color. Each card also compares the paint with photos of the real thing it is named after. Studio lighting also matters. Each value is the lit side of the paint, and each card shows how the same paint reads on its shadow side.

A color is consistent when the ad's hue and chroma fall inside the range the photos span, and at the edge when it misses by 2 or less. Lightness is left out because in photos it mostly follows the exposure. That is why ΔE and the verdict can disagree. Amarillo mangó has a large ΔE mostly because the ad is lighter than every mango photo.

Every color leans the same way

In all five colorful ones, the ad sits at a higher hue angle than the real thing, by 6° to 29°. The reds read more orange, the yellow a little greener, and the green and the turquesa bluer. One of the five, frambuesa, rests on a single photo.

The ad's whites and blacks read neutral. The white cone, the white bricks and the white shape under the yellow stay within 4 of zero on a* and b*, and so do the black backgrounds. So there is no overall color cast.

A tint error in the tape transfer would do exactly this, but these photos can't prove it. The paints were never meant to be exact copies of the things they are named after, and one tint error would push every hue by about the same angle, while these range from 6° to 29°.

How the values were measured

  1. Source. The .mp4 file (H.264, 1920×1440, BT.709, limited range). The .webm file (VP9) went through the same steps as a cross-check, and the two agree within ΔE 0.03 for every color.
  2. Scenes. ffmpeg scene detection found the cuts. About 5 frames were dropped at each cut, and every second frame in between was used. Turquesa uses the two splash shots from 35.9 to 40.0 s, which include your screenshot. The pouring shot after them is lit brighter and was left out.
  3. Decoding. Raw Y'CbCr planes were converted to R'G'B' in floating point with the BT.709 matrix. The result matches ffmpeg's own conversion within 0.5 of a code value. The screenshots were only used to find the scenes, because they pass through the player's color management and JPEG compression.
  4. Paint pixels. Pixels were kept when their hue and chroma matched the paint (by lightness alone for the white). The mask was shrunk by a few pixels so edges that blend into the background drop out, and clipped highlights were removed.
  5. The value. Only the 60th to 95th percentile of lightness was kept, for every color. That keeps the lit side of the paint and drops the shadow side and the gloss highlights. The chosen value is the median of what remains, taken in CIELAB. The band was picked by comparing candidates against lit patches in your screenshots.
  6. The real things. No official source publishes measured colors for these subjects. For the flamboyán, the Floral Reflectance Database has no entry, no Royal Horticultural Society chart code turned up, and the pigment studies measure extracts, not petals. So each subject was measured from real photos on Wikimedia Commons. Each photo has camera EXIF data, was taken between 2006 and 2021, and comes from a different photographer. A hand-drawn box marks the subject, and inside it the same hue and chroma mask, lit band and Lab median as the video apply. Each subject was defined before the search, and photos that did not fit were dropped, such as deep water for the turquesa. For the flamboyán, using only the 8 photos with the fewest blown-out pixels gives a value within ΔE 2.5.
  7. Conversions. Lab and LCH use D50 with Bradford adaptation, the same as Photoshop and CSS. CMYK uses the Adobe SWOP v2 and GRACoL 2006 profiles with relative colorimetric intent and black point compensation. ΔE is CIEDE2000 after a round trip through the profile.
