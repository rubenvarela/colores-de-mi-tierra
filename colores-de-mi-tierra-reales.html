"Ad vs real" is consistent when the ad's hue and chroma fall inside the range the photos span, and at the edge when it misses by 2 or less. Lightness is left out because in photos it mostly follows the exposure.

Photos are not a lab measurement. Each camera sets its own white balance and exposure, so each value is the median across photographers, and the squares on each card show the spread. Blanco coco and azul adoquines are nearly gray, so white balance decides most of their value. Verde quenepa rests on 3 photographers and frambuesa piragua on an estimate checked against 1 photo.

Is there a common factor?

Yes, in hue. The video stores color as brightness plus a two-axis color plane. Measured in that plane, every colorful ad color is rotated the same way from the real thing. The rotation is 5.9° for amarillo mangó, 14.6° for turquesa and 18.9° for rojo flamboyán. Verde quenepa is the outlier at 43.5°, from 3 photos. Frambuesa sits at 10.2° from its single photo. The three colors with solid photo sets average 13.2°, give or take 5.4°.

Saturation and brightness have no common factor. Rojo flamboyán's saturation in the ad is 0.66 of the flower's, mango's is 0.76 and turquesa's is 1.63 times the water's. So only the hue rotation carries from one color to another.

Each of the three was tested with the rotation fitted from the other two. Removing that lean moves every one toward its photos.

Mango overcorrects to 72.7°, just below its photo range. Its ΔE barely moves because most of its gap is lightness, which the rotation does not touch.

The lean is shared, not specific to reds. Rojo flamboyán alone leans 18.9°, and removing that much from frambuesa overshoots to #C4332F, ΔE 5.6 from the piragua photo. The shared 13.2° gives #C03621, ΔE 2.5. The piragua photo played no part in fitting the factor, so that is an independent check.

This still does not prove a tint error in the tape transfer. The factor was fitted against the real things, not the original paints, so it mixes transfer error with how the paints were designed.

Which code to trust

1HEX and RGB

The master for each color. The photos and the video are both sRGB, so HEX is the measured value rounded to 8 bits.

2HSL and HSB

The RGB numbers re-expressed as hue, saturation and lightness. Nothing is added or lost beyond rounding.

3Lab, LCH, OKLCH

Exact math from sRGB into device-independent spaces. Give the Lab value to a paint store, since their color-matching spectrophotometers read in Lab.

4CMYK

The least accurate. CMYK depends on ink, paper and press, so the numbers change with the ICC profile. ΔE shows how far the print lands from the screen color. Below 2 is hard to see.

How the values were measured

  1. Primary values. No official source publishes measured colors for these subjects, so each one was measured from real photos on Wikimedia Commons. Each photo has camera EXIF data, was taken between 2006 and 2021, and comes from a different photographer. Each subject was defined before the search, and photos that did not fit were dropped, such as deep water for the turquesa. A hand-drawn box marks the subject. Inside it, pixels are kept by hue and chroma, blown-out pixels are removed, the lit side is kept (the 60th to 95th percentile of lightness) and the median is taken in CIELAB. The primary is the median across photos.
  2. Frambuesa piragua. Commons has only one usable piragua photo, so its primary is an estimate. The ad value was rotated back by 13.2° in the video's color plane, the average lean of amarillo mangó, rojo flamboyán and turquesa. Only hue changes. The one photo then serves as the check.
  3. Ad values (alternates). Decoded from the .mp4 file with the BT.709 matrix in floating point, cross-checked against the .webm file within ΔE 0.03, and sampled on the lit side of the paint in each scene.
  4. Conversions. Lab and LCH use D50 with Bradford adaptation, the same as Photoshop and CSS. CMYK uses the Adobe SWOP v2 and GRACoL 2006 profiles with relative colorimetric intent and black point compensation. ΔE is CIEDE2000.
